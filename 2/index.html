When I finished high school at fifteen, my mom asked what I wanted to do with my life. I said I wanted someone to pay me to have good ideas. She said she didn't think that was a job. I worked my way through publishing, music, film, photography and fashion until I found out it was: creative director. 25 years after first holding the title, I am one today, sort of. Color, form, tactility and quality still matter to me, but I think of them as a given in how anything should be made. Now I bring that thinking to something larger: the systems we live inside, and the creativity underneath them.

iris.marketFounder 2025—

iris.market is my trust-based, friends-of-friends marketplace for stuff, skills, space and time — cash optional. I started it after a storage unit full of things I couldn't name or price made me realize how much idle resource sits untracked in everyone's life. iris helps you use what you have to get what you need.

ENORMECo-founder and CEO 2025— · Ettore Sottsass, 1985

ENORME began in 1985 as a postmodern telephone designed by Ettore Sottsass with David Kelley and backed by Jean Pigozzi. In 2026, we resurrected the original 1985 Delaware corporation — now six partners — continuing its mission of merging technology with radical design, led by Memphis Group member Marco Zanini. We also just stewarded the acquisition of Masanori Umeda's Tawaraya Ring, originally owned by Karl Lagerfeld, by the Museum of Modern Art.

Everybody.WorldCo-founder and Creative Director 2016–2025

Carolina Crespo and I co-founded Everybody.World in November 2016, launched on $100k my mom loaned us and 11 years at American Apparel. I'd been let go twice and used the time to help the garment workers organize a union, then went back to what actually inspired me: old Colors magazines, Memphis Group design, manufacturing, turning trash into treasure.

The idea: invite everyday extraordinary people to design the pieces missing from their lives, and pay them 10% of every sale. Jean Pigozzi sketched an eight-foot snake-shaped body pillow. Prakash Gokalchand, who I'd watched play chess in MacArthur Park for years, designed an easy long-sleeve shirt and the softest sweatpants. We made the Trash Tee — 100% recycled cotton, an industry first — and built out basics in recycled fleece, jersey and wovens, all made in the Americas. Lorde, André 3000, the Whitney, Google, Twitter and half of tech later used our blanks for their own merch.

No institutional money, no debt, no ad spend — just word of mouth and press, and it grew to $6 million a year. Carolina still runs it today, profitably, bills always paid, 55 cents of every dollar going back into local manufacturing and textile recycling.

American ApparelGlobal Creative Director 2004–2015

I spent eleven years at American Apparel, ending up Global Creative Director in my twenties — high-waisted jeans, tube socks, the disco pant, the deep V-neck. “I single-handedly destroyed West Hollywood,” I like to joke; you'd see a guy at La Cienega and Santa Monica with his shirt pulled down to his belly button and think, what have we done. I joined when it was 1,000 employees and two stores, and helped grow it into the largest sewing factory in North America — Made in USA, vertically integrated, nearly 300 stores in 13 countries, 10,000 employees worldwide. It defined a generation, in advertising and in culture, for better or worse, and it taught me everything I know about building things at scale.

FreshjiveCreative Director 2001–2003

I raved through the mid-'90s in Freshjive tees and JNCOs, so landing a job there in 2001 was surreal. Rick Klotz — the brand's founder, a radical creative and gifted artist with a sick sense of humor — called me Irie. Andrew Hogge, later DJ Lovefingers, was a graphic designer there alongside me. It's where I got my first Creative Director title, producing The Freshjive Propagandist, and where I met Dov Charney, who slept on Rick's floor when he first moved to LA.

I put everything I had into that publication: Danielle Levitt, the late Shawn Mortensen, a painter working from inside a French prison, a prolific crochet artist I found on Craigslist. We launched the issue at MOCA's Geffen space before it was even built out, and hired a Richard Ramirez lookalike to do portraits with guests. Those years were a blast.

Civics

As a fourth-generation Angeleno and fifth-generation Californian, I love this city and this state. I'm especially interested in communication, technology and design as they shape how we reach all citizens, so needs get met and resources don't go to waste.

MacArthur Park

In 2007 I moved into the American Cement Building and started the MacArthur Park Preservation Society, documenting Westlake — a neighborhood at the geographic and historical heart of LA, and one of its most under-resourced. Its character comes through anyway.

In 2018 I joined the board of Levitt Los Angeles, the free public bandshell in MacArthur Park founded by the late Liz Levitt. We booked mainstream and obscure acts alike, and the stage brought out every kind of Angeleno.

State PoliticsRestricted folder

In December 2024, California's Chief Service Officer Josh Fryday invited me to write a white paper for Governor Newsom's office, from the perspective of a small business owner and brand builder. That led to a pro-bono Creative Director role for the then-frontrunner in the California governor's race. I still volunteer and stay in the conversation on local and state politics, bringing what I've learned running businesses into the room.
